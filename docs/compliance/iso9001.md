# ISO 9001:2026 — Clause Support

Which ISO 9001:2026 clauses uqmes performs, which it evidences, and which remain yours. The certificate is your organisation's; this page is the map you bring to the audit.

## Overview

ISO 9001 is the foundation for quality management systems. The aerospace and
automotive standards build on it.

!!! info "This page follows ISO 9001:2026"
    **ISO 9001:2026 was published on 16 September 2026**, with a transition period
    expected to run to **September 2029**. Certificates to the 2015 edition stay
    valid through that window, so if you are certified today you are not behind —
    you are in transition.

    It is a measured revision, not a rewrite: the clause structure is the same and
    most requirements carry over. What changed is summarised under each affected
    clause below, and only **one** change has any software component — clause 6.3,
    which now reaches across the whole life of a change rather than its planning.

    The rest — quality culture and ethical behaviour in 5.1.1, the risk and
    opportunity split in 6.1, climate change in 4.1 and 4.2, and broader treatment
    of organisational knowledge — sit in the management clauses, where no
    application is the mechanism. They are marked ➖ below for that reason, not
    because they are gaps.

This page maps the standard clause by clause. It is not a readiness score: the
certificate is awarded to **your organisation**, and most of what an auditor
examines is how you run the business, not which software you bought.

| | Meaning |
|---|---------|
| ✅ **Provides** | uqmes is the mechanism. Point an auditor at the software. |
| 🟡 **Partial** | uqmes does some of it; the rest is a setting you configure or a step you take. |
| 📋 **Evidences** | **You** run the activity — uqmes holds the records that prove you ran it, in the form an auditor asks for. |
| ➖ **Yours** | A management or facility activity with no software component. Listed so you can see it is accounted for, not because it is missing. |

!!! info "Why clauses 4–6 are on this page"
    A quality management system is a management system. Clauses 4, 5 and 6 —
    context, leadership, policy, objectives and risk — are things an
    organisation does, and no software performs them. They are listed here
    anyway, because an auditor will ask about them and because uqmes usually
    holds the **evidence** that you did: who was authorised to do what, which
    policy revision was current and who had it, what changed and why.

    Nothing in these clauses is a shortfall in the software. Read ➖ as
    *accounted for, owned by you*.

## Clause 5: Leadership

No software can demonstrate leadership commitment. What it can do is hold the
records that show the commitment took effect.

| Clause | Requirement | Status | Evidence |
|--------|-------------|--------|----------|
| 5.1 | Leadership and commitment | ➖ | Yours. Management participation shows up in the approval records — who signed off what, and when — but the commitment itself is not a software artifact. **New in 2026:** 5.1.1 adds promoting a **quality culture** and **ethical behaviour**, echoed in 7.3 Awareness and 7.1.4. No application is the mechanism for any of it. |
| 5.2 | Quality policy | 📋 | You write the policy. Hold it in **Documents**: it then carries a revision, an approval and a release date, so you can show which policy is current. uqmes keeps no distribution register of who holds it — see [Document Control](document-control.md). |
| 5.3 | Roles, responsibilities and authorities | 📋 | uqmes is the system of record for this. `TenantGroup` / `UserRole` define who holds which authority, the 15 role presets document the intended split, and the audit log shows every change to a group's permissions with actor and timestamp. |

## Clause 6: Planning

| Clause | Requirement | Status | Evidence |
|--------|-------------|--------|----------|
| 6.1 | Actions to address risks and opportunities | 📋 | You run the risk assessment. uqmes holds the treatment and the proof it worked: preventive CAPAs, RCA records, and effectiveness verification. There is no risk register model — keep the register itself in Documents. **Changed in 2026:** 6.1 is now split into 6.1.1–6.1.3, separating determination of risks and opportunities from the actions taken, with expanded guidance in Annex A. The split sharpens what the register has to show; it does not change what the software holds. |
| 6.2 | Quality objectives and planning to achieve them | 📋 | You set the objectives. The quality dashboard measures against them — first-pass yield, open NCRs, overdue CAPAs, supplier reject rate, on-time delivery — so the monitoring the clause requires is already running. Targets are not stored in uqmes. |
| 6.3 | Planning of changes | 🟡 | Change control: `ProcessChangeRequest` → `ProcessChangeOrder` → Notice. The request carries `proposed_change`, `justification` and `risk_analysis`; the order carries the `implementation_plan` and effective date; the notice carries what changed and what affected parties must do, routed to them by notification rules. Approval precedes release, and the approval lock prevents a change reaching production any other way. **Changed in 2026 — see below.** |

!!! warning "6.3 now covers the whole life of a change — and this is the one gap"
    The 2026 edition extends clause 6.3 past planning. On top of purpose,
    consequences, QMS integrity, resources and responsibilities, it now asks you to
    consider **how the change will be communicated**, **how its effectiveness will
    be monitored and evaluated**, and **how the results will be reviewed**.

    Three of those four are covered. Purpose and consequences are on the request.
    Communication is the **Notice** stage — what changed, when it takes effect, what
    affected parties must do, routed by notification rules on release. Effectiveness
    is captured at closure as a narrative.

    **What is missing is the criteria.** 2026 asks you to decide *how you will judge
    whether it worked* while you are still planning, then review against that. Today
    the closure narrative is written after the outcome is known, which is a weaker
    control — it cannot fail, because the standard it is measured against is written
    last.

    Until the system captures it, **write the success criteria into the change
    request's justification** when you raise it, and refer back to them at closure.
    That satisfies the clause with the fields that exist. Where a CAPA drove the
    change, say so and point at its effectiveness verification rather than
    duplicating it.

### 7.1.5.2 Measurement Traceability

| Requirement | Status | Evidence |
|-------------|--------|----------|
| Measurement definitions | ✅ | MeasurementDefinition with nominal, tolerances, units |
| Measurement results | ✅ | MeasurementResult linked to definitions |
| SPC baselines | ✅ | SPCBaseline tracks control limits with audit |
| **Equipment found unfit — are earlier results affected?** | ✅ | The gauge's **exposure list**: every quality report it was used on between its last passing calibration and the check that failed, exportable |

!!! important "This is the half of 7.1.5.2 auditors actually probe"
    The clause does not stop at keeping gauges calibrated. It asks that **when
    equipment is found unfit for purpose, you determine whether the validity of
    previous measurement results has been adversely affected, and take
    appropriate action.**

    A failed calibration sets the gauge out of service, which protects the
    *next* part and says nothing about the ones already measured — a gauge
    drifts during the interval rather than failing the instant it is checked.

    From a calibration record that found the gauge unfit, **What it measured**
    lists everything it touched in the suspect window, with the window stated:
    *"Measured between {last passing calibration} and {failed check}."* The list
    exports.

    Three details worth knowing before you cite it:

    - **"Unfit" includes a gauge that was adjusted back into tolerance**, not
      just one that failed outright — which is the commoner real case.
    - **It does not act on the list.** Re-inspecting, accepting on other
      evidence, telling a customer or raising an NCR are judgements for a
      person. The clause requires the assessment; the action is yours.
    - The link appears only once a gauge has a failing calibration recorded.

    Related control, same clause: a reading against a gauge that is out of
    service, or whose last calibration **failed**, is refused on every path —
    operator capture, the measurements API, bulk recording, and an edit to a
    reading that names the gauge — with *Equipment '&lt;name&gt;' is OUT_OF_SERVICE (failed or
    missing calibration). Recalibrate or pick a different gauge.* A failed gauge
    cannot be returned to service without a passing record. A gauge that is only
    **overdue** still records the reading; operator capture shows a warning.

### 8.6 Release of Products and Services

| Requirement | Status | Evidence |
|-------------|--------|----------|
| QA approval | ✅ | A `QaApproval` records the release of a step for a work order: the signer, the server's time, the originating IP, the verification method, and a server-derived meaning — *"QA release of {step} for {work order}"*, never supplied by a client |
| Step-level signoff | ✅ | `requires_qa_signoff` gates the step; the process author chooses how firmly it is signed (`qa_signoff_assurance` — check off, or signature + password), the same choice a substep's sign-off block makes |
| Segregation of duties | 🟡 | Whoever worked the step on units still at it — signed one of its substeps, or is assigned to or completed the unit's open visit to the step — cannot release it, enforced at every assurance level. Bounded: units that have already moved on, and voided substep completions, fall outside it — see [Signatures](signatures.md) |
| FPI verification | ✅ | A first piece can release the step instead; **pass and waive are both held to the step's authored assurance**, so the gate cannot be walked around by releasing the other way |

!!! note "Older releases read as what they were"
    A `QaApproval` made before assurance was authored carries verification
    method *None* and no verified time. That is the honest record — it was made
    under the signer's session alone — and it must not be presented as a
    verified signature.

### 8.7 Control of Nonconforming Outputs

| Requirement | Status | Evidence |
|-------------|--------|----------|
| Identification | ✅ | QUARANTINED part status, visible on every queue |
| Preventing unintended **delivery** | ✅ | A quarantined part is excluded from shipping outright — it cannot leave the building. While a part has an open disposition, its status cannot be changed by editing the part or by the bulk status action — *&lt;part&gt; has an open disposition (&lt;DISP-…&gt;); its status changes through that disposition.* The disposition's decision moves it |
| Preventing unintended **use** | 🟡 | Per-step: a step blocks a quarantined part only where **Block on quarantine** is set on it. **Off by default** — see below |
| Disposition | ✅ | QuarantineDisposition with REWORK / REPAIR / SCRAP / USE_AS_IS / RETURN_TO_SUPPLIER |
| **On whose authority** (§8.7.2 c) | ✅ | Recording the decision needs `approve_disposition`; where the inspector lacks it, an approver co-signs **at the inspector's station** with their own password and the record carries **their** name. `decision_authorized_by` and `decision_authorized_at` are the pair 8.7 asks for |
| Concession for accepting nonconformity | ✅ | USE_AS_IS and REPAIR are **refused without a recorded approval reference** — a hard stop, not a warning. The reference, date and authoriser are stored, and once the disposition is decided the concession record (Requires Approval, Approval Received, Approval Reference, Approval Date) cannot be edited — a new Decide brings its own. Severity can be raised after the decision but not lowered |
| Containment before closure | ✅ | A Critical or Major disposition cannot close until a containment action is described. The close check needs the containment text only; marking containment complete, which stamps who and when, is a separate step it does not require |
| Scrap verification | ✅ | Whether scrap was verified, **by what method**, by whom and when — the control that stops scrapped product re-entering the supply chain |
| Rework tracking | ✅ | `rework_attempt_at_step` counter, with escalation after repeated attempts |

!!! warning "Segregation from further processing is a per-step setting"
    8.7 asks you to control nonconforming output "to prevent its unintended use
    or delivery". **Delivery is prevented unconditionally** — a quarantined part
    can never ship.

    **Use is not.** A quarantined part keeps advancing through steps unless a
    step carries **Block on quarantine**, which is off unless somebody set it.

    If your procedure says a held part stops dead, arm that flag on the
    operations where it matters, and be ready to show an auditor which steps
    have it. Physical segregation remains yours either way — the system holds
    the record, not the cage.

### 9.3 Management Review

ISO 9001 §9.3.2 lists what a management review must take as input. uqmes
supplies most of that list as live data rather than as a figure someone
assembled by hand the week before.

| Requirement | Status | Evidence |
|-------------|--------|----------|
| Review inputs (§9.3.2 c) | 📋 | §9.3.2 c lists seven trends the review must consider. The quality dashboard carries **six of them live**: customer satisfaction (complaint CAPAs and on-time delivery), process performance and conformity, nonconformities and corrective actions, monitoring and measurement results, audit results (CAPAs of type Internal Audit), and external-provider performance (supplier scorecards). The seventh — *the extent to which quality objectives have been met* — is not there, because objectives and their targets are deliberately not stored in uqmes (see 6.2). |
| The other inputs (§9.3.2 a, b, d, e, f) | ➖ | Yours. Previous review actions, changes in context, resource adequacy, the effectiveness of risk actions and improvement opportunities are management judgements, not data a system holds. |
| Running the review, and its minutes | ➖ | Yours. uqmes does not model a meeting. Hold the minutes and the resulting decisions in Documents so they are controlled and auditable. |

| Requirement | Status | Evidence |
|-------------|--------|----------|
| Effectiveness verification | ✅ | CapaVerification with `effectiveness_result` — **CONFIRMED**, **NOT_EFFECTIVE** or **INCONCLUSIVE**. Inconclusive is the default, so a verification opened and never concluded reads as undecided rather than as a pass |
